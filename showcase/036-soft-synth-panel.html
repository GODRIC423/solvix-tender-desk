Why one grey

The SS-36 is pressed from a single sheet of grey polymer. Nothing is printed on and nothing is glued down: every knob, key and window is the same surface pushed up or pressed in, lit from the upper left as though it were sitting on a bench by a north-facing window.

That constraint decides the whole instrument. A control is raised when you may touch it and sunk when it is only reporting back to you. The one blue in the room belongs to the signal path — arcs, lamps, the scope trace — so your eye follows the sound rather than the furniture.

Playing it

Press Power first: the audio engine is only built on a deliberate gesture, never before. The engine lamp lights, the scope wakes and the readout swaps standby for the current patch.

Knob caps respond to a vertical drag — up to open, down to close — and to a horizontal one if that suits your wrist better. Hold Shift while dragging for a quarter-speed trim, or double-click a cap to snap it back to the factory position. Every control is reachable by keyboard: tab to a cap and use the arrow keys, Page Up or Down for a coarse sweep, Home and End for the extremes.

Signal path

A single oscillator plus a square sub feeds one resonant low-pass filter, which is shared by all sounding notes; each note carries its own gate-driven amplitude contour before the master stage.

Notes are polyphonic up to whatever your machine will bear. Hold three keys and open the cutoff slowly — the filter is common to the chord, so the whole stack brightens together, which is exactly the behaviour of the old dividing-down organs this panel is quoting.
