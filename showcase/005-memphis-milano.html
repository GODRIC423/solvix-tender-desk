01

A record stuck on repeat

Storia · 1980–1988

On 11 December 1980, Ettore Sottsass, sixty-three years old and already famous for the Olivetti Valentine typewriter, invited a handful of much younger designers to his apartment on Via San Galdino in Milan. Michele De Lucchi, Matteo Thun, Marco Zanini, Aldo Cibic, Martine Bedin, George Sowden and Nathalie Du Pasquier drank, argued and sketched while Dylan’s “Stuck Inside of Mobile with the Memphis Blues Again” played over and over on the record player. The song gave the group its name; the argument gave it its purpose.

They were bored by the good taste of Italian modernism: chrome, leather, black, beige, and the endless sermon about function. Memphis proposed the opposite. Plastic laminate, the material of diner counters and cheap kitchens, was promoted to a luxury surface. Patterns were lifted from 1950s coffee bars, Art Deco, comic strips and the printed circuit board. Colour was applied without hierarchy: a bookcase could be pink, teal and banana yellow at once and hold almost no books.

The first collection was unveiled on 18 September 1981 at the Arc ’74 showroom on Corso Europa, during the Salone del Mobile. Around 2,500 people crushed into the street outside. Critics were baffled, sales were tiny, and the influence was enormous: within two years Memphis shapes were on record sleeves, in MTV idents, in shopping malls and in Pee-wee’s Playhouse. Sottsass walked away in 1985, complaining that the joke had hardened into a style. The group formally dissolved in 1988. The pieces are still made in Milan today.

02

Six pieces that misbehave

Sei pezzi · laminate, wood, glass, nerve
1

Carlton

Ettore Sottsass1981Bookcase

The poster child. A totem of laminated fibreboard whose shelves lean at angles no book ever asked for. Sottsass called it a room divider; almost everyone who owns one calls it a sculpture. The base wears the Bacterio print and the whole thing stands 196 cm tall, like a small, cheerful person with its arms out.

2

Tahiti

Ettore Sottsass1981Table lamp

A lamp that looks like a duck, or a flamingo, or a small bird with opinions. A yellow beak of a shade sits on a pink stem, planted in a Bacterio-print base. It gives very little light and enormous pleasure, which was roughly the point of the whole enterprise.

3

Casablanca

Ettore Sottsass1981Sideboard

A sideboard with its arms flung open. Covered entirely in Bacterio laminate, its diagonal wings hold glasses and bottles you now have to reach for at an angle. It was the most photographed piece of the 1981 show and is still the most argued about, usually by people who have to dust it.

4

Super

Martine Bedin1981Floor lamp

Bedin, the youngest founder at twenty-four, drew a lamp you could take for a walk: a half-moon body on rubber wheels with coloured bulbs sprouting like antennae, trailing its cord behind it like a leash. She called it a pet, and it is the only Memphis object that looks like it might follow you home.

5

Bel Air

Peter Shire1982Armchair

From the Los Angeles wing of the group: an armchair assembled from a shark’s fin, a beach ball, a rolled-up towel and a wedge of swimming pool. Every arm is a different shape and the back is a fin. It is more comfortable than it looks, which, admittedly, is not saying a great deal.

6

First

Michele De Lucchi1983Chair

The Memphis chair that actually sold. A steel hoop floats behind a round seat, carrying two wooden spheres and a flat disc for your shoulders: a diagram of a chair drawn by someone who has just seen an orrery. Still in production, still cheerfully unreasonable, still surprisingly all right to sit on.

03

From Bacterio to Bowie

Cronologia · 1978–2016
  1. 1978

    Bacterio is bornSottsass designs a laminate for Abet Laminati: wormy black marks scattered on white, inspired by a photograph of bacteria under a microscope. It becomes the unofficial flag of everything that follows.

  2. 11 Dec 1980

    The founding nightAt Via San Galdino the record sticks, the wine runs out and the name is chosen before dawn. Barbara Radice agrees to be art director; the youngest person present is twenty-three.

  3. 18 Sep 1981

    Debut at Arc ’74Fifty-five pieces of furniture, lighting, ceramics and textiles go on show at Corso Europa during the Salone del Mobile. Some 2,500 visitors block the street. Carlton, Casablanca and Tahiti are all in the room.

  4. 1982

    The guests arrivePeter Shire, Michael Graves, Shiro Kuramata, Javier Mariscal and Hans Hollein contribute to the second collection. Memphis is now an international movement with a Milanese postcode.

  5. 1983

    Pop absorbs itDe Lucchi’s First chair becomes the group’s bestseller. Memphis shapes appear in MTV idents and fashion shoots; Karl Lagerfeld furnishes his Monte Carlo apartment with nothing else.

  6. 1985

    Sottsass leavesThe founder walks out, arguing that Memphis has become exactly what it set out to mock: a style. The others continue for three more collections.

  7. 1988

    The group dissolvesMemphis formally ends after seven years and roughly 300 objects. Production of the catalogue continues under the Memphis Milano company, which still makes the pieces to the original specifications.

  8. 1991

    Lagerfeld sells upThe entire contents of the Monte Carlo flat go under the hammer at Sotheby’s Monaco. Lagerfeld later says he had simply finished with it, the way you finish with a haircut.

  9. 2016

    Bowie’s collectionMore than 100 Memphis pieces from David Bowie’s estate sell at Sotheby’s London. A Carlton and a Casablanca both fetch several times their estimates, and the squiggle returns to the front pages.

Eat some cake.

Memphis lasted seven years and never made much money. It did make the case that a shelf can be funny, that a lamp can have a personality, and that the rules of good taste are just somebody else’s furniture. This page borrows its palette from the 1981 catalogue, its type from a poster nobody printed, and its manners from the Carlton.

The pieces shown here are still produced by Memphis Milano. Drawings are stylised, not scale models. Bacterio and the terrazzo band are hand-coded SVG tiles.
