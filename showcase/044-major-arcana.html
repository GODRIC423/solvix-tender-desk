On the Method

Why three cards

The three-card spread is the oldest and plainest of the tarot's arrangements. It asks nothing of the reader but attention. One card for the ground you stand on, one for the moment, one for the direction the moment is leaning. Read left to right, it becomes a sentence; read together, it becomes a weather report for the inner life.

Only the twenty-two major arcana are used here. These are the deck's great archetypes, the Fool through the World, and they speak in larger movements than the numbered suits. A reading drawn from them alone tends to be about turnings rather than errands.

Every card is dealt upright. Reversals have their place, but a first reading is better without the double negatives.

On the emblems

The pictures on these cards are not reproductions of any historical deck. Each is an original reduction of the card's traditional image to a few lines: the Magician's lemniscate, the Priestess between her pillars, the Wheel with its eight spokes, the Tower under its single stroke of lightning. Midnight ink on bone, with gold where the light falls.

The interpretations were written for this deck and are deliberately brief. A tarot card is a mirror, not a memo; the text is there to tilt the mirror, and you supply the face. Nothing here predicts. The cards are shuffled by your own browser and no reading is recorded or sent anywhere.

Sit with each card before turning the next. The pause is part of the reading.
