About the diagram

Thirty‑six stations. Five lines. Nothing to scale.

Halcyon is a flat city that has never existed, so its metro can be drawn honestly: every segment runs at nought, forty‑five or ninety degrees, every interval is even, and the river has been politely asked to leave. What survives is the part that matters — the order of the stops and where you change.

    Halcyon Line

    Westgate Yards → Kestrel End

    The original 1901 route: dockside to ridge, and still the fastest way through the middle of the city. It climbs the escarpment between Old Salt Quay and Meridian Cross on the network's only sustained gradient, which is why the trains sound like they are trying.

    Stations9
    End to end24 min
    Peak headway3 min

    Cobalt Line

    Thornfield → Marsh End

    The north–south spine, deep-level through the centre and on the surface everywhere else. Between Foundry Gate and Elm & Sixth it runs in a cutting so straight that drivers report seeing daylight at both ends at once.

    Stations10
    End to end27 min
    Peak headway4 min

    Verdant Line

    Larkspur Gate → Eastern Gate

    One long diagonal from the botanic gates down to the coast road, drawn at forty-five degrees for its entire western half. The newest line, and the only one where every platform is level with its train.

    Stations7
    End to end19 min
    Peak headway5 min

    Amber Line

    Highfield → Halcyon Docks

    The orbital: down the western ridge, round the corner at Chalkmill and along the entire southern waterfront. Slow, scenic, and the only service that runs through the night at weekends.

    Stations10
    End to end31 min
    Peak headway6 min

    Lilac Line

    Cypress Green → Halcyon Docks

    The eastern connector. It stitches four lines together without once entering Zone 1, which makes it the cheapest way to cross the city if you are patient and travelling between two outer stations.

    End to end18 min

    03

    Travel notes

    1

    Change at Meridian Cross

    It is the only three-line interchange on the network and every platform sits on one level. Fulcrum Square looks closer on the diagram, but the walk between its halls is four minutes longer than the map suggests.

    2

    The diagram is not a map

    Distances here are drawn for legibility. Stations are spaced evenly whatever the ground does between them, and no line ever bends at an angle you could not fold a sheet of paper into.

    3

    Zone 1 is smaller than it looks

    The dashed ring contains five stations only. If your journey begins and ends outside it, the Lilac Line will carry you round the east and keep you out of the higher fare entirely.

    4

    Last trains and night service

    Services leave the outer termini at 00:20 and the centre at 00:48. On Friday and Saturday the Amber Line runs half-hourly all night between Highfield and Halcyon Docks.
