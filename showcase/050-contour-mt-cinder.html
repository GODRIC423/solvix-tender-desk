Field notes

Mt. Cinder is a young compound cone squatting inside the breached rim of a much older caldera. Everything above the 1600 metre index line is loose scoria; everything below it is glacial till, larch, and the braided outwash of Cinder Basin. The summit crater holds Ash Lake, a shallow acid tarn that turns the colour of weak tea after autumn rain and freezes by the second week of October.

The Redline Traverse leaves the basin road at the old pumice quarry and climbs the south-east flank in a long series of switchbacks cut by the survey party of 1968. Above Obsidian Notch the marked route follows the rim anticlockwise; in cloud, the cairns are the only reliable guide, since the fall line on either side of the rim looks identical and one of them ends in the crater.

    Contours on this sheet were derived from an airborne lidar sweep and re-drawn by machine at a hundred metre interval; index contours carry their value in metres above the Cinder Basin vertical datum. Streams are traced down the steepest local gradient and are shown where flow was observed in August. Shaded patches drifting across the sheet are cloud shadow, not vegetation.
