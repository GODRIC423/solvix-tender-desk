The Grand Theatre —

Eight Distinct Acts

Engaged at Enormous Expense! — First Appearance in this Town of every Artiste.

Programme

The Band, under the direction of Herr Anselm Rieger, will play at Seven

1

Professor Quill’s

Mechanical Orchestra

Forty brass automata, wound by a single key, performing the Overture to Zampa without one living hand upon them. The Professor stands aside and takes snuff.

Twelve years in the building at the Professor’s workshop in Clerkenwell, the Orchestra comprises cornets, clarionets, a bass drum, and a cymbalist whose expression of concentration has been much remarked upon. Each figure is dressed in scarlet and gilt after the Household Bands.

Ladies and Gentlemen are cordially invited to the footlights after the piece to inspect the mechanism, and to satisfy themselves that no confederate is concealed within the drum.

2

The Sisters Marchetti

Equilibrists of the Slack Wire

Three young ladies from Bologna who dine, dance a quadrille and pour tea upon a rope no thicker than a gentleman’s thumb, twenty feet above the stalls.

The Sisters conclude with the celebrated Passage of the Chairs, in which the eldest, Signorina Lucia, carries her two sisters across the wire seated upon kitchen chairs balanced on her shoulders. No net is employed. The Management has, however, placed a mattress.

3

Signor Belforte

Ventriloquist & Polyphonist

Voices from the flies, the cellar, the gallery, and a locked portmanteau. A conversation with seven persons, none of whom are present.

Signor Belforte will imitate, by request, the following: a saw-mill in full operation; a quarrel between two cabmen; a nursery of infants at feeding-time; and the late Mr. Gladstone addressing the House. Requests may be handed to the Acting Manager before the performance, written legibly.

4

Miss Harriet Dunmore

The Nightingale of Lambeth

Ballads, sentimental and comic, including her own composition The Lamp-lighter’s Daughter, which has been sung in every parlour from Deptford to Dundee.

Miss Dunmore is accompanied on the harmonium by her mother. The song-sheet, price one penny, may be had of the attendants, and the Audience is respectfully requested to join in the chorus of the final verse only, and not, as at Tuesday’s performance, in the preceding six.

An Interval of Fifteen Minutes

Ices, Bottled Ales & Ginger Beer in the Saloon · Oranges from the Gallery are not to be thrown

5

The Great Vanderhoof

The Iron Coffer of Amsterdam

Manacled, sacked, and padlocked into a chest forged by the Dutch Admiralty, the Great Vanderhoof will free himself in under four minutes by the theatre clock, or forfeit fifty pounds.

A committee of six gentlemen from the audience will be invited upon the stage to examine the chest, apply the locks, and hold the keys. Sailors, locksmiths and members of the constabulary are particularly welcome. The forfeit has been paid once, at Hull, in 1886, owing to damp.

6

Mr. Tobias Wrenn & Co.

in the Screaming Farce

“The Lodger Who Would Not Leave”

A landlady, a lodger, four doors, and a policeman who arrives at every wrong moment. Twenty minutes; fourteen exits; one bath-tub.

Mrs. Pargeter, a landlady — Miss Adeline Foy. Mr. Cadwallader, the lodger — Mr. Tobias Wrenn. Constable Budge — Mr. Wrenn also, by a rapid change of whiskers. The Bath — itself. Written by Mr. Wrenn in a single afternoon, and it shows, gloriously.

7

Madame Zorayda

Second Sight & Thought Reading

Blindfolded with the Manager’s own handkerchief, Madame will name the contents of your pockets, the date upon your coins, and the name you were called at school.

Madame Zorayda is assisted by her nephew, Master Felix, aged eleven, who walks among the Audience and asks only that objects be held up plainly. Persons of a nervous disposition are advised that nothing whatever is revealed that a lady would not wish revealed. Gentlemen are given no such assurance.

8

Grand Finale — The Whole Strength of the Company

Britannia!

Living Statuary & Tableaux Vivants

Six classical groups in marble and bronze, revealed one by one in the new Limelight, concluding with Britannia Triumphant and the National Anthem, sung by the entire House standing.

The groups: The Wrestlers; Ajax Defying the Lightning; The Death of Nelson; Industry Crowned by Commerce; Andromeda (Ladies’ Night only); and Britannia, with real lion, hired. The Limelight apparatus is under the charge of Mr. Prout, late of the Alhambra, and the House is assured it is entirely safe.

  • Upper Boxes2s.
  • Pit1s.
  • Gallery6d.

Half-Price at Nine o’clock to Dress Circle and Stalls only. Children under twelve, half-price to all parts except the Gallery. Seats may be booked at the Box Office daily from Eleven till Four, without extra charge.

Doors open at Seven — Commence at Half-past Seven precisely

Rules of the House

  1. Smoking is strictly prohibited in the Auditorium. Cigars may be enjoyed in the Saloon and the Gallery Corridor only.
  2. Ladies in the Dress Circle and Stalls are earnestly requested to remove their bonnets, that those behind may see the stage as well as the milliner’s art.
  3. Children in arms are not admitted to any part of the House on any pretext whatever.
  4. No money will be returned after the rising of the curtain, nor upon the plea of not liking the performance.
  5. The Management reserves the right to alter or vary the Programme should illness or the railway company render it necessary.
  6. Persons standing in the gangways will be requested to sit down; persons declining will be requested to leave.
  7. Whistling, cat-calls and the throwing of nut-shells are the province of the Gallery, and are to be confined there.
  8. Complaints should be made at once to the Acting Manager, Mr. E. Pruitt, who may be found in the Vestibule and who will receive them with fortitude.
