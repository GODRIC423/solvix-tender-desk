Currentsa field of drifting things

Every walker on this plate is blind. Each one only ever asks the field, which way here? — and the answer is a single number from a Perlin function written by hand, below. A hundred thousand such answers a second make the weather you are looking at.

Seed — Walkers — Octaves 03 Drifting

On noise,
and the shape of drift

The field is a function

There is no wind here and no water. A flow field is nothing but a function that takes a point on the plane and returns an angle. Ask it at every pixel and you get a map of directions; drop a particle onto that map, let it step forward along whatever angle it lands on, and it draws its own path. Everything you can see on this page is that one idea, repeated about a hundred and forty thousand times a second.

Why Perlin, not random

If the angle at each point were simply random, the walkers would jitter in place and the plate would turn to grey fuzz. What is needed is randomness that remembers its neighbours — smooth, continuous, unpredictable at a distance but coherent up close. Ken Perlin built exactly that for the film Tron in 1983, and it remains the workhorse of procedural texture. The version running here is written from scratch in about forty lines: a permutation table shuffled by the seed, a quintic fade curve, gradient dot products at the eight corners of a lattice cell, trilinear interpolation between them.

The recipe, in five moves

  1. Seed the table. A small integer hash generates the shuffle, so the same seed always rebuilds the same field.
  2. Stack three octaves. Each is twice the frequency and half the amplitude of the last — fractal Brownian motion, which adds grain without losing the large gesture.
  3. Turn value into angle. The noise, in the range −1 to 1, is multiplied by three π. Small changes in scale rotate the whole weather system.
  4. Walk and record. Every walker strokes a hairline between its last position and its next, at very low opacity. Density is only patience made visible.
  5. Drift the third axis. The noise is sampled in 3D; the third coordinate creeps forward each frame, so the field itself slowly breathes.

Reading the dials

Seed
Rebuilds the permutation table. Step it one at a time to watch a family of related fields, or shuffle for a stranger.
Scale
How far apart the lattice points sit. Low is a slow ocean swell; high is turbulence and hair.
Density
How many walkers are alive at once. Fewer reads as drawing; more reads as fog.
Palette
Cream is the house ink. Ember pushes coral to the front. Tide cools the whole plate toward the indigo it sits on.
Save
Writes the canvas exactly as you see it to a PNG, at full device resolution, stamped with its seed so the plate can be found again.
