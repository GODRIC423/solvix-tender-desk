Editorial / rant / whatever

Nobody asked for issue three

The Anchor shut in July. You already know this because you were either there on the last night or you were the person who lied about being there on the last night. Two hundred people in a room built for ninety, the ceiling sweating, the bass player from Kettle Fever handing out plastic cups of something that was mostly lime cordial. Then Monday came and there was a padlock on the door and a laminated sign about "exciting residential opportunities."

Here is the bit nobody wants to type out. The Anchor did not die because of the council, or the developers, or the noise complaint from the flat upstairs. It died because for two years we treated it like a utility. It was just there, like a tap. We went to the big-name shows at the Academy and paid fourteen quid for a lager and told ourselves we would get down to the Anchor "next week." Next week is now a hoarding with a picture of a woman laughing at a salad.

So this issue is not a tribute. Tributes are for things that are finished. This issue is a list of who is still playing, where they are doing it, and what it costs to get in. Most of the answers are a community hall, a Wednesday, and three pounds. That is not a tragedy. That is how it always started.

Print this. Copy it. Leave it on the bus. If you are reading copy number forty-one, someone photocopied us, which is the highest compliment available in this postcode.

— ed. (typed with two fingers, corrected with none)

Interview 01 — recorded in a van

Gutter Hymnal

Three-piece from the estate behind the retail park. Two guitars, no bass, a drummer called Deacon who is not religious. Their tape Sermon On The Ring Road sold out of thirty copies in one night at the Anchor. We spoke to them while they waited for a man called Pav to bring a jump lead.

Why no bass player?
Mags: We had one. He moved to Leeds to do a master's in something. We tuned the second guitar down so far it started to feel like a personality. Now people ask us why we haven't got one and we say it's a concept. It is not a concept.
The tape sold out. Are you pressing more?
Deacon: We have four blank C60s and a dubbing deck that eats every third one. So, statistically, three more. Bring your own tape and we'll do it in front of you. That's the deal. That's the whole label.
What does the ring road have to do with it?
Mags: Everything on that tape was written walking home along it at two in the morning because the last bus is a rumour. You write differently when a lorry is going past every forty seconds. Shorter songs. Louder.
Where are you playing now the Anchor's gone?
Deacon: The scout hut on Ferrers Lane has a hatch where they sell squash. Rowan from Civic Damage sorted it. The floor bounces. We are not sure it's meant to.

Interview 02 — kitchen table

Kettle Fever

Two sisters, one borrowed synth, one drum machine from a car boot sale that only keeps time when it is warm. They play fast, then faster, then the drum machine crashes and they finish the song by stamping. Nobody minds.

Is the drum machine a member of the band?
Fen: Legally, no. Emotionally, it is the one we argue with most. It has a preset called "LATIN 2" that we have never heard the end of, because it dies at about ninety seconds. We wrote all our songs to be eighty-five seconds long. Problem solved.
People call you a punk band. Do you?
Iris: We call ourselves a band that owns one distortion pedal between two people and has to pass it across the stage. If that's punk then yes. If punk means having the right jacket then no, we share that as well.
Where does the name come from?
Fen: Our nan. Whenever the house got too loud she'd say she had kettle fever, and go and stand in the kitchen with the kettle on so she couldn't hear us. She came to one show. She stood by the tea urn the entire time. She said it was very good, considering.
What's next?
Iris: A song that lasts ninety-one seconds, out of spite. And the Wednesday at the community centre. Bring a blanket for the drum machine.
Interview 03 — bus shelter

The Civic Damage

Five people and a trombone, which is technically a sixth person. They organise more shows than they play, which is how the scout hut, the community centre and the allotment society hall all ended up on this issue's gig list. Rowan does the talking. Rowan does most things.

A trombone. Explain.
Rowan: Bex played it at school and it was in the loft. We tried it on one song as a joke and it turned out a trombone through a fuzz pedal sounds like a building falling over slowly. We were never going to un-hear that. Now it's on everything.
You've booked half the gig list yourself. Why?
Rowan: Because if you wait for someone to open a venue for you, you wait for a very long time and then it's a coffee shop. A hall with a hatch and a caretaker who wants everyone out by half ten is a venue. Every one of those halls has a booking form. Nobody reads them. We read them.
What do the caretakers make of you?
Bex: Derek at Ferrers Lane came to watch us soundcheck and said, "Is it all like that?" We said yes. He said, "Fair enough," and turned the heating off to save money. He has been to every show since. He stands at the back with his arms folded. We love Derek.
Any advice for someone starting a band this week?
Rowan: Book the show before you have the songs. The date will write them for you. And bring your own extension lead, every single time, forever.

Letters / complaints / one apology

Dear Riot Spine

Issue two said Gutter Hymnal were "the loudest band in the borough." I have measured this. I own a meter. They are the third loudest. The loudest is the man who does the shutters at the market at 5am. Please print a correction.

— T. Okafor, by the market

Correction printed. Man with the shutters, get in touch, we'll do an interview.

You spelled my sister's name wrong and put the wrong date for our show, and we had eleven people turn up on a Tuesday to a locked door. They stayed. We played in the car park. It was one of the best nights of my life. Please do not do this again.

— Fen, Kettle Fever

Sorry. Genuinely. That's the apology. The car park show is the reason we started the gig list.

Why is it always the same six bands? There are other people making music in this town who don't have a trombone or a broken drum machine.

— name withheld, "a real musician"

Send us a tape. We have literally never received a tape from anyone we haven't printed. Not once. Pigeonhole's in the record shop.

Copy number 38 was missing page 5. I still read it. I have now worked out what The Civic Damage said from context and I think I prefer my version.

— Priya, top deck of the 51

Keep it. It's rarer than the ones with the page in.

How this was made

Do it yourself, badly, tonight

Four sheets of A4, folded, stapled twice with a long-arm stapler borrowed from the school office and never returned. Body text typed on an Olympia that drops its lower-case e. Headlines in marker on the back of a cereal box. Everything stuck down at whatever angle it landed, because straightening things is how you end up with a newsletter.

Photocopied at the library, 3p a side, on the machine that adds the grey stripe down the left. The stripe is now part of the design. The halftone on the photos is what happens when you copy a copy of a copy. We recommend it.

If you can read this, you can make one. Two staples, one idea, forty friends.
