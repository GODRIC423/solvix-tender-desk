How the solar share works

One roof, ninety-six slices of sun.

A solar share is not a stock certificate and not a subscription. It is a slice of an actual array bolted to an actual roof three floors above your kitchen, plus a say in how the surplus gets spent. Here is the whole loop, start to finish.

  1. 1

    Buy a slice

    One share is 500 watts of panel — roughly three panels' worth — priced at cost. Households can hold up to four shares; a hardship fund covers the first share for anyone on the building's social tenancy list.

    £310 one-off
  2. 2

    The roof makes power

    Every panel feeds one shared inverter room, so nobody's slice gets the shady corner. On a clear September day the array makes about 240 kWh; in December a tenth of that. The cellar battery smooths the evening.

    48 kW · 120 kWh stored
  3. 3

    Credits land on your bill

    Each month we split the metered generation by shares held and credit it against members' electricity bills through the building's single supply point. Last year a one-share household saved £118.

    Monthly credit
  4. 4

    Surplus grows the garden

    Power we export while everyone's at work is sold, and members vote each spring on where it goes. So far: the drip irrigation, two beehives, the cider press and a winter polytunnel.

    Voted every April

Where the water came from

The roof drinks about 90,000 litres a season. Rain tanks fill first; the solar pump only lifts mains water on the driest weeks. This year the tanks carried us through until the third week of August — nine days longer than last year, thanks to the mulch crew.

Upcoming workdays

Bring gloves. We bring soup.

Workdays are how the roof gets done and how neighbours become members. Nobody signs up; you just come up the fire stairs. Children welcome, dogs on the lower terrace, and the stair lift runs for anyone who needs it. Lunch is from the beds and the battery-powered kettle never stops.

Membership

Own a piece of the weather.

Verdant Commons is a registered community benefit society. One member, one vote, whatever you hold. Membership is open to anyone living within the four buildings connected to our supply point, and to the school across the courtyard.
