    Mayor's log

      Almanac · how the plot works

      A hundred tiles, five ways to fill them, one very short day.

      Everything on the plot above is an ordinary HTML box: three faces, one shadow, a couple of CSS variables. Nothing is drawn on a canvas, so you can inspect a tower, hover its windows and watch the sun paint a different face every hour.

      Zoning

        Light

        A full day lasts ninety seconds at normal speed. The sun rises at six in the corner of the plot, climbs over the rooftops until noon, and sets at six in the evening on the opposite side. Every face of every cube is shaded by how squarely it looks at the sun, so the bright side of town swaps from east to west while you build.

        Shadows are cast from each footprint along the sun's direction and stretch out to more than twice a building's height near dawn and dusk. When the sky turns coral the windows begin to glow, the corner-shop signs light up and the moon takes over the arc until morning.

        Drag the slider in the time dock to scrub the day by hand, or pause it to keep a favourite hour. The badge in the header counts each midnight you sit through.

        Controls

        • KeyboardFocus the plot and use the arrow keys to move the dashed cursor. Enter or Space places the selected building, Backspace clears a tile, and 1–6 switch tools.
        • TimeP toggles the cycle, [ and ] nudge the clock by an hour.
        • Happiness is a blend of green space and employment:
        happiness = 30 + 40 × green ratio + 30 × jobs coverage

        Green ratio compares parks with homes, capped at one park for every two homes. Jobs coverage is the share of residents who can find work in a shop or a tower. Your plot is saved in this browser between visits.
