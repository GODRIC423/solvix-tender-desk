A split day · 38.7° N, 9.1° W

Two Cities,
One Clock

Lisbon and Auckland sit almost exactly opposite each other on the face of the clock. For a few weeks each spring and autumn, when both cities keep summer time, the gap is precisely twelve hours; the rest of the year it is eleven.

Scroll to walk through one Lisbon day. The night beside it belongs to Auckland, and it runs the other way.

0700

Bica, standing

The first 28 tram grinds up Rua da Graça with its bell going. At the counter of a pastelaria on Rua da Madalena the coffee comes small, black and hot, drunk on your feet, with a pastel de nata the baker pulled out twenty minutes ago. The Tagus is still the colour of tin.

1000

Laundry light

By ten the sun has cleared the castle hill and the Alfama is strung with washing. The Elevador da Bica hauls its yellow car up towards Bairro Alto for the fortieth time this morning. Tour groups gather at the Sé; the locals cut through the alleys they know.

1300

Sardines and shade

Lunch is grilled sardines on bread at a table under an awning, the smoke drifting across the Mercado da Ribeira. The light is white and total. Shutters come down for two hours; the azulejos throw the glare straight back at anyone who stays outside.

1600

Miradouro hour

At four the heat softens and people drift to the miradouros: Santa Catarina, Senhora do Monte, anywhere with a wall to sit on and the river below. A Super Bock sweats in the sun. Across the water the Cristo Rei holds out his arms over Almada.

1900

Golden hour, fado warming up

The sun swings out over the Atlantic and the whole city turns the colour of this side of the page. In Alfama a guitarra portuguesa is being tuned behind a half-open door. Dinner is still two hours off; this is the hour for a walk down to the river.

2200

Dinner, finally

Bairro Alto eats late. Bacalhau à Brás, a carafe of vinho verde, a shot of ginjinha from the hatch on Largo de São Domingos on the way home. The trams have thinned to one every twenty minutes, and the river has gone back to the colour of tin.

End of the Lisbon day

Neither city waits

    The Same Minute, Half a World Later

    Everything on this side is happening at the very same moment as the events opposite. The minute hand is shared; only the hour hand splits.

    This column runs the opposite direction. Scroll down and Auckland's day arrives from above to meet Lisbon's morning.

    1900

    Ferries home

    The Devonport ferry noses back into Britomart under a Sky Tower already lit. On Ponsonby Road the tables are filling; someone has ordered the whole snapper. Out past the Harbour Bridge the Waitematā has gone from green to ink.

    2200

    Last call on K’ Road

    Karangahape Road is loud in the way only a Thursday can be. The last western-line train pulls out of Britomart; on the bridge the headlights run in pairs towards the North Shore. In the suburbs the tūī have stopped, and the kitchens have gone dark.

    0100

    The harbour breathes

    The city is asleep except for the port. Cranes swing containers off a ship from Tauranga under floodlights, and across the Domain a ruru, the morepork, calls twice and is answered. The tide is turning under the wharves at Wynyard.

    0400

    Flour and diesel

    The bakers on Dominion Road have the ovens up and the first loaves out. At Westhaven, fishing boats slip their moorings and motor out past North Head. Rangitoto is a shape, then a darker shape, against a sky that is not yet grey.

    0700

    Sunrise over Rangitoto

    The sun comes up behind Rangitoto and runs straight down Tamaki Drive, where the joggers have already been out for an hour. The first ferries cross from Waiheke. Flat whites appear on every corner, and the city gets on with its Friday.

    1000

    Market morning

    Mid-morning in the Domain: the Wintergardens are open and the glasshouse is sweating. At La Cigale the queue for croissants curls round the corner. Someone in a Lisbon kitchen is going to bed; someone in an Auckland kitchen is only just deciding on lunch.
