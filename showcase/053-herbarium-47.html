  1. Corolla nodding

    Bell of five lobes fused for two-thirds their length, rose-pink paling to white at the throat, five darker veins. Opens after six in the evening; faint scent of clove.

  2. Cauline leaves

    Two opposite pairs, linear-lanceolate, entire, sessile & glaucous beneath — the upper pair much reduced.

  3. Basal rosette

    Ovate, obscurely crenate, long-petioled; mostly withered by flowering time. One leaf already browning — kept by good fortune.

  4. Root perennial

    Slender taproot with fibrous laterals; the crown throws one to three shoots a year. Washed in the combe stream before pressing.

  1. Bud erect

    Held upright until the day it opens, then pendent. Calyx teeth linear and longer than in Campanula rotundifolia, which it is easily taken for when closed.

  2. Capsule

    The fruit turns upward as it ripens — an ovoid capsule opening by three pores at the base. Seeds glossy, pale brown, about 0.6 mm.

  3. Stem

    Slender, flexuous, glabrous, faintly ridged; 18–35 cm. Usually single, rarely branched from the base. Bleeds a clear sap when cut.

From the collector's field book

Sunday, 14th June 1931 — Hollin Combe

Walked up from the combe by the drove road and reached the scarp a little after five, wind in the west and the turf still wet from the morning's rain. Found the colony where the sheep track crosses the old lynchet: perhaps forty plants in a band not thirty paces long, and none elsewhere though I searched as far as the beech hanger.

The bells were shut when I arrived and I sat with the flask until, at about ten past six, they began to open one after another, quite quickly, the way a room fills with people. A small Bombus was working them before the last had opened. Pressed two; this is the better. The pink is truer than Marlowe's plate suggests, though it dulls in the press to something nearer rose-madder.

Not in Bentham. Not, I think, in anything. Return next year for seed, and write to Marlowe.

— I. M. H.

About this sheet

A single gathering, read four times over ninety years.

Sheet 47 holds the plant from which the Dusk-bell was described: the type. Everything later botanists know about the species begins with this one pressed stem, its typed label, and the pencil notes that have accumulated on the sheet since 1931. The washes of colour are the collector's own, added the evening the plant was pressed, before the pink could fade.
