  1. Nothing here is in a hurry.
  2. The warm rises because it is warm,
  3. not because it was told to.
  4. Watch one shape lean toward another
  5. and take a whole minute to decide.
  6. They do not touch. They become.
  7. Somewhere a clock is losing its argument.
  8. You are allowed to be this slow.
  9. Let the thing you are waiting for
  10. find you already softened.
  11. What melts is not lost;
  12. it is only changing its mind about edges.
  13. Stay. The light will come around.
  14. It always comes around.

How to sit with the goo

1

Do not read ahead

Each line arrives roughly every four seconds, which is slower than you want it to be. That gap is the whole exercise. Let a line land, notice the urge to know the next one, and stay with the blobs until it comes on its own.

2

Follow one blob

Pick a single shape and watch it for the length of a line. It never stops moving, and it never hurries. When it meets a neighbour the two merge without a seam, sit together for a while, then separate as if nothing had been decided.

3

Let it come to you

Rest your cursor somewhere and wait. The goo is attracted to it, slowly, the way warm wax leans toward the lamp. If you chase the blobs they will always be a little behind you. If you hold still, they gather.

A lava lamp is a very simple machine: heat, wax, time. The wax rises because it is warm and sinks because it has cooled, and nothing about that cycle can be rushed without ruining it. This page borrows that patience and adds fourteen lines to it.

The visual is built from nine soft circles that drift on slow sine waves and get pulled gently toward wherever your pointer rests. On their own they would simply overlap. What makes them merge is an SVG filter: the layer is blurred, then its alpha channel is pushed through a colour matrix that turns the blurry halo back into a solid shape. Where two circles are close, their blurs add up and the threshold joins them into one form, so the shapes appear to melt together. A second, lighter blur is applied afterwards so the result never gains a hard edge; the lamp is meant to glow, not to cut.

If your system asks for reduced motion the drift stops, the poem is shown whole, and the goo becomes a still pool of colour. You can also switch to the whole poem at any time with the button above, or step through it with the arrow keys.
