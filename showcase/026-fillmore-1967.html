Three nights · one bill

The Bill

Friday, January 13

The Velvet Orchard

Twelve-string raga blues, two drummers, one very long song.

Seven people from the Mission who rehearse in a fruit warehouse and sound like it: sweet, bruised, slightly fermented. Their sets begin as folk songs and end forty minutes later somewhere off the coast of Bombay. Come early. They tune for a long time and the tuning is part of it.

Saturday, January 14

Moonshot Caravan

Fuzz organ, harmonica, and a bass player who never stops smiling.

Up from Los Angeles in a repainted bread truck, the Caravan play hard, fast and cheerfully — dance music for people who have forgotten how their legs work. Expect the Farfisa to be run through a tape echo until it howls, and expect the room to answer back.

Sunday, January 15

Sister Lantern & The Glass Choir

Four voices, a harmonium, a cello, and lights that breathe with them.

Sunday is the quiet night. Sister Lantern sings in a register just above a whisper and the Choir stacks harmonies behind her like colored glass held up to a window. Aurora Liquid Light project oils and dyes directly onto the singers; the whole stage melts, slowly, on purpose.

Particulars

Come As You Are

Tickets are three dollars in advance at the Psychedelic Shop, City Lights, and the box office on Geary; three-fifty at the door, if there is a door left by then. One ticket covers one night. A weekend pass is nine dollars and comes with an apple.

Doors open at eight on Friday and Saturday and at seven on Sunday. The hall holds a little over a thousand people, standing. There are chairs along the walls for the tired and the balcony is open for the shy.

Light is by Aurora Liquid Light — four overhead projectors, colored oils, dyes, water, clock faces and a bicycle wheel. Their work is improvised each night alongside the music and is never repeated. Bring your eyes.

Sound will be loud on Saturday and soft on Sunday. Friday is undetermined. Free apples and cold cider are in the lobby all three nights, courtesy of the Orchard's uncle.

Please leave your car, your watch and your cynicism at home. Flowers welcome. Dogs on Sunday only. The poster you are looking at is the ticket stub of the future; keep it flat.
