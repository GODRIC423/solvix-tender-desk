Light for the dark half of the year.

From November to February this coast gets seven usable hours of daylight, and most of them are the colour of wet newspaper. We make three lamps for the other seventeen.

Pull the dimmer below and the afternoon moves with it.

Mørketid — the murk timeDarkness is not a problem to be solved. It is a room to be furnished.

Danish has a plain word for the season when the sun gives up early: mørketid. It is not poetic. It is the entry on the calendar between the last bicycle ride home in daylight and the first one in spring.

The reflex is to fight it — to flood the ceiling with 4000 kelvin and pretend it is eleven in the morning. That produces a room like a pharmacy: bright, correct, and impossible to sit in. Everybody stays at the kitchen table with their coats half off.

Hygge is the opposite instinct. You accept that it is dark at four and you place three small warm sources at the height of a seated person: one over the table, one beside the chair, one on the sill for the street to see. The corners are allowed to stay dim. The room shrinks to the size of the people in it, which is the whole point.

Every lamp we make is dimmable to two per cent, because the last two per cent is where the evening actually happens. Nothing here is decorated. Birch is left the colour birch is, wool is left undyed, the cord is the cord.

Four honest materials

Nothing is stained to look like something else. Everything can be taken apart with one screwdriver and sent back to us when it wears out.

Birch, oiled once

Cut in Småland, air-dried eighteen months, finished with a single coat of linseed oil. It yellows a little each winter, which we consider correct.

Undyed Gotland wool

Felted at 1.4 mm — thick enough to hold the shade's shape, thin enough that the seams glow faintly when the lamp is low.

Opal glass

Two layers, blown into a wooden mould. The inner white layer scatters the filament so you can look straight at the lamp from a chair.

Enamel, colour “hav”

One muted blue, mixed once a year in a fifty-litre batch. When it runs out the run ends; we do not match old batches.
