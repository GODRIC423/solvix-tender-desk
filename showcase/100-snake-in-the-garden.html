Snake in the Garden

There is a snake in my garden and she is always hungry. Feed her the apples. Do not let her touch the fence, and do not let her bite her own tail, because that is rude.

Rules of the garden

  1. Apples are for eating. Each one makes her one square longer and a little bit faster.
  2. The fence is for keeping the rabbits out, not for headbutting. Touching it ends the drawing.
  3. Her own tail counts as the fence. She forgets this constantly.
  4. The grass is only grass. You may drive straight over it and nothing will happen.

Field notes

1

The real snake

The snake who actually lives at the bottom of our garden is a grass snake. She is olive coloured with a yellow collar and she is not the slightest bit interested in apples.

What she eats is frogs, newts and the occasional slow worm, and she swallows them whole and head first because she has no way of cutting anything up. In October she goes down under the compost heap and does not come out again until the ground is warm, which is usually about the same week the first bees turn up.

2

The apple tree

Ours is a Bramley and it leans badly to the south because of the wind coming over the allotments. Bramleys are cooking apples, so if you bite one raw your whole face folds up.

The windfalls are the important ones. They go soft and brown in the grass and every wasp for half a mile knows about it within a day. We are supposed to collect them in a bucket every evening. We do this roughly twice a summer. The rest of the crop goes to the birds, the wasps and, once, a very determined badger.

3

How it was drawn

Nothing on this sheet is a straight line. Every stroke in the garden is drawn three or four times over, each pass nudged a pixel or two off course, so the wax looks like it caught on the tooth of the paper.

Then the whole picture is redrawn nine or ten times a second with fresh wobble, which animators call boiling. If you have asked your computer for less motion, the wobble is frozen and the drawing simply sits still, which is also fine.
