Ceremony & celebration

The Day

  1. Two o’clockThe ceremony, in the long barn. Do be seated by a quarter to; June is famously punctual and Theo has promised to be.
  2. Three o’clockChampagne and Rye Bay oysters in the orchard, weather permitting. If it rains, we drink in the threshing barn and pretend we planned it.
  3. Five o’clockDinner under the beams at one long table. Places will be set; the seating plan is letterpressed too, so please forgive us if we cannot move you.
  4. Half past eightSpeeches, mercifully short, then the first dance. The band is a seven-piece from Hastings who know everything your parents like and most of what you do.
  5. MidnightCarriages. The last shuttle to Rye leaves the lower field at a quarter past twelve, and the lane is very dark, so please take it.

Dress

Garden formal. The orchard grass is soft after June rain and heels sink; linen, silk, and something you can dance in. The barn cools after dark — a shawl or a jacket will be earned.

Staying

Rooms are held at The George and at the Mermaid Inn in Rye until the first of April; ask for the Okafor–Ellison party. Both are a short, cobbled stumble from the shuttle stop.

Little ones

We adore your children, but this is a grown-up evening and a late one. The exception is babes in arms, who may attend in any dress they please.

Gifts

Your presence is the present. Should you wish to give something more, a contribution toward two weeks of walking in the Azores would be treasured; there will be a quiet card at the barn.

A note on finding us

The Way to the Barn

By train

London St Pancras to Ashford International, then the little Marshlink line to Rye: about an hour and twenty in all. A shuttle waits on the station forecourt at one o’clock and again at half past.

By car

Leave the A268 at Peasmarsh church and follow the lane a mile and a half past the hop gardens. The barn is on the left after the ford. Please do not trust the satnav past the church; it wants to take you through the river.
