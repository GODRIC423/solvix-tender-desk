Workers' Radio Hour

Every day from 05.30 to midnight the tower above Foundry No. 3 throws eight broadcasts across the river, the tram sheds and the barracks. One transmitter. One frequency. Every loudspeaker in the district.

Station RKV went on the air in the winter of 1926 from a lattice mast bolted to the roof of Foundry No. 3. Its first broadcast was a shift roster read aloud by a crane operator. Its second was a choir. It has not been quiet since.

We do not broadcast to the district. We broadcast from it. Every presenter on this page still holds a shop-floor card: the newsreader pours steel, the engineer keeps the turbines, the playwright welds tram rails by day.

Loudspeaker sets have been installed in fourteen canteens, six reading rooms, the tram depot, the bathhouse and the children's home on Gorky Lane. Where there is no loudspeaker there is a crystal set, and where there is no crystal set there is a comrade with a diagram and a spool of wire.

Frequency 1350

Transmitter power 4 kilowatts. Range: the whole district on a still night, and half of the next one when the wind is right.

The Whistle Blows

Presented by Anfisa Belova · Crane Operator, Bay 2

The day's shift roster, the river level, the weather over the marshalling yards and one verse sung badly on purpose so nobody goes back to sleep. Belova reads the roster twice: once fast, once slow enough to copy down.

№2
07.0030 min

Steel & Bread

Presented by Pavel Gruzdev · Furnaceman

News from the shop floor and the co-operative store: tonnage poured, bread quotas met or missed, and the grievances sent in by the night shift on the backs of ration cards. Gruzdev reads every one, including the rude ones.

№3
09.0050 min

The Literacy Hour

Presented by Vera Shtein · Pattern Shop

Thirty-three letters, taken one a week, then whole words, then a newspaper. Listeners write along with a pencil on whatever is to hand. By spring the reading rooms report that the pencils are being replaced by pens.

№4
12.0025 min

Midday Chorus

Foundry Choir No. 3 · Conductor Ilya Marchenko

Forty voices in overalls, sung live from the canteen with the trays still clattering. Requests are taken by shouting them through the window of the transmitter hut. Marchenko conducts with a ladle.

№5
14.3035 min

The Engineer's Question Box

Presented by Nadezhda Orlova · Turbine Hall

Why does the lamp dim when the rolling mill starts? Can a tram run on the same current as a kettle? How long is a metre of wire, really? Orlova answers with chalk you can hear squeaking and a patience you can hear running out.

№6
17.0020 min

Tram-Line Dispatches

Presented by Semyon Volk · Depot Dispatcher

Live from a telephone nailed to the wall of the depot: which lines are running, which are frozen, which driver has decided to take the long way past the bakery. The only programme in the district that people set their watches against.

№7
20.0060 min

Theatre of the Wireless

The Blue Blouse Collective · Written by Olga Rud

A new play every fortnight, performed in the transmitter hut with a bucket of gravel for footsteps and a sheet of tin for the storm. This month: The Girder, a comedy in three shifts about a beam that refuses to be lifted.

№8
23.00Until sign-off

Night Signal

Presented by Danil Krupin · Night Watchman

Slow music from a gramophone with one good needle, barge reports from the river station, and the last word to the night shift: who is on, who is off, who is owed a favour. Sign-off is the whistle, played backwards.

Listen!

1350kilocycles · 222 metres · 4 kW
  1. Find a loudspeaker. Canteens, reading rooms, the depot, the bathhouse. The sets are switched on by whoever arrives first.
  2. Or build a crystal set. Diagrams are pinned in every reading room. One coil, one cat's whisker, one pair of headphones, forty metres of aerial strung to anything tall.
  3. Tune to 1350. Turn until the whistle is loud and the hiss is quiet. If you hear a choir, you have found us. If you hear a waltz, you have found the other lot.

RKV · 1350

Station RKV, Foundry Tower Wireless, Krasnogorsk. A fictional station and an imaginary district, set down in the manner of the 1920s posters that shouted from every wall. Programme correct as of the spring roster.

Letters, requests and grievances to the transmitter hut, roof of Foundry No. 3, or shouted through the window during Midday Chorus.
