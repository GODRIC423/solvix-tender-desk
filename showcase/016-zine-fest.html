03 Venue

The Old Print Hall

Address

14 Drum Lane
Ropewalk Quarter

The hall is the long brick building with the pink-and-blue door. If the door is grey, you are at the tyre place next door; they are lovely but sell no zines.

Getting there

Tram — line 3 to Ropewalk, then a four-minute walk following the stencilled arrows on the pavement. Bus — the 44 and the night bus N44 both stop outside (see table 04 for a review of the N44). Bike — racks in the loading bay, watched by the coffee cart.

Access

Level entrance, step-free hall, accessible toilet by the stage. Aisles are a minimum of 1.5 m. The hall is loud at peak times; Sunday 11:00–12:00 is a quiet hour with lights low and no readings. Assistance dogs welcome. Email the committee if you need a reserved seat for the readings.

Bring

Cash in small notes, a tote, and something to swap at 15:00 on Saturday. Don't bring a stroller if you can avoid it — there is a pram park by the door.

04 How to table

Get a table, sell your pages

Applications for 2026 close on Sunday 20 September. We read every one. There is no jury of taste — we care that it's yours, that it's finished, and that you'll actually turn up.

  1. Make the thing

    Any format, any print method, any run size. A zine that exists in three copies is a zine. A PDF with a "coming soon" is not, yet.

  2. Fill in the form

    Name, zine title, one line about it (the same line we print in the table above), format, and a photo of a physical copy. Two minutes, honestly.

  3. Hear back by 1 October

    We confirm by email with your table number, load-in time, and the exhibitor pack. Half-tables are shared with a stranger who becomes a friend.

  4. Turn up and set out

    Load-in from 08:30 Saturday. Tables are 180 × 60 cm with two chairs. Bring your own stand, sign, float and someone to cover your loo breaks.

05 Colophon

Printed slightly wrong, on purpose

This page is set in Bricolage Grotesque and "printed" in two passes: fluorescent pink first, riso blue second. Where the two layers overlap they multiply into a purple no single ink could make. The blue drum never quite lines up with the pink one, which is how you know a human loaded the paper.

Zine Fest is organised by a committee of seven who met at a photocopier. Since 2019 we have printed 11,400 sheets, jammed the machine 212 times, and never once run a straight edge. Posters this year are riso on 120 gsm recycled, and they will fade in a sunny window. Good.
