01

on form and function

excerpt from the exhibition text, 1923 / 2023

a chair is not a picture of sitting. it is sitting made visible — a circle for the hand, a square for the floor, a triangle for the load.

in the summer of 1923 the school in weimar opened its doors and asked a simple question of every object it had made: what does this do, and does it look like it? the lamps, the cradle, the tea infuser and the house at the end of the road were not decorated. they were resolved. ornament was not forbidden so much as found unnecessary once the task had been understood.

the three primitives on this page are not a logo. they are the working alphabet of the workshops. the circle is the wheel, the plate, the lamp shade; the square is the wall, the window, the printed page; the triangle is the roof, the bracket, the wedge that holds a joint tight. red for the shape that moves, blue for the shape that rests, yellow for the shape that points. that pairing was argued over in the preliminary course and never fully settled, and it is still worth arguing about.

a hundred years later the argument has changed sides. form now follows function so obediently that it has become a style of its own, and the exhibition asks whether the primitives can still surprise us. so the shapes here do not stay put. scroll, and they rearrange themselves — a diagonal for the poster, a column for the argument, a row for the timetable, a stack for the door. the same three parts, four different jobs.

gallery text, room 1 · after the 1923 statement of principles

  • the object must explain its own use.if a handle needs a label, the handle is wrong.
  • the machine is a collaborator, not a threat.repeatable form is honest form; the mould is a kind of signature.
  • lowercase is enough.why write with two alphabets when one will do the work?

program of lectures

bauhaus week · 15 – 19 august · haus am horn

tuesday 15 august

  • the opening address returns to the 1923 slogan and asks what "technology" meant to a school with one working lathe. 60 min · lecture hall · free with ticket

  • a three-hour reconstruction of the famous questionnaire that asked students to assign red, yellow and blue to the circle, triangle and square — and to defend the choice. 180 min · workshop 2 · 24 places · 8 € materials

  • the one built house of the 1923 exhibition, walked room by room as the sun leaves the central living space. 75 min · meet at the garden gate · 15 places

wednesday 16 august

  • on herbert bayer's lowercase experiment of 1925, why it failed as a typeface and succeeded as an argument, and what it means for screens. 60 min · lecture hall

  • participants build costumes from card, wire and the three primitives, then rehearse a five-minute figure on the exhibition stage. 150 min · stage · 16 places · all ages

thursday 17 august · late opening

  • a tour of the reconstructed benches with the objects placed back where they were made: the tea infuser next to the lathe, the slatted chair next to the saw. 90 min · 20 places

  • the closing debate of bauhaus week. the phrase that was never said at the bauhaus, and the buildings it has been used to excuse. 90 min · lecture hall · followed by a reception

saturday 19 august

  • one shape per pass, three passes per poster. take home a diagonal of your own. 120 min · print room · 20 places · free

getting there

from weimar central station, bus 1 to am horn (12 min) or a twenty-minute walk through the park an der ilm, past goethe's garden house.

the workshops are 400 m from the house along a signed, step-free path.

access

the ground floor of the house and all of the workshops are wheelchair accessible. the upper floor is reached by a narrow original staircase.

large-print gallery texts and a tactile model of the plan are at the entrance.
