01Pour-over

Medium-fine

Time
3:00–3:30

Rinse the filter, bloom with twice the coffee's weight in water, then pour in slow spirals. The 1:16 window gives a cup that tastes like the origin rather than the method. Go to 1:15 for more body, 1:17 to open up floral, tea-like roasts.

4:00 steep

Immersion is patient: every ground sits in every drop for four full minutes, so a slightly tighter ratio keeps the cup from going thin. Break the crust at four minutes, skim, wait another few, and press only as far as the grounds — never through them.

25–30 s

Here the ratio is weighed on the way out, not in: 18 g of dry coffee should yield about 36 g of liquid espresso. Shorter pulls toward 1:1.5 taste thick and chocolatey; longer toward 1:2.5 turn brighter and more transparent. Change the grind, not the dose, to hit your time.

Grind
Medium
Water
85 °C
Time
1:30 + press

A concentrated brew you can dilute to taste: 15 g of coffee and 180 g of water, stirred, steeped ninety seconds and pressed gently for thirty. Drink it as is for a punchy cup, or top it up with hot water to a pour-over strength. Cooler water flatters this one.

05Cold brew

Smooth · low acid · overnight
1:8coffee : water
Grind
Extra coarse
Water
Room / fridge
Time
14–18 h

Time replaces heat. Grind coarse, combine with cool water at 1:8, and leave it alone for most of a day. What you decant is a concentrate; pour it one-to-one over ice or with milk. For a ready-to-drink batch, brew at 1:15 and skip the dilution.

Weigh, don't scoop.

A tablespoon of light-roast beans can weigh 40 % more than a tablespoon of dark. A five-dollar scale removes the guesswork and makes every number on this page repeatable. Ratios shown are starting points from the Specialty Coffee Association's brewing guidelines and common café practice; adjust by half a step at a time.
