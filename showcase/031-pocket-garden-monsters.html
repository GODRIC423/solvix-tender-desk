RULES OF THE LAWN

A round lasts forty-five seconds. Catch ten bugs before the timer in the top-right corner reaches zero and the round is won; the seconds you have left become your record.

Four bugs share the lawn at any time. Each catch draws the next species from a shuffled deck of six, so every species appears before any repeats. Newly arrived bugs blink for half a second and cannot be caught while they settle.

Some bugs fight back. Snails hide in their shell when the net comes close. Beetles feel you coming and bolt. Read the bestiary below before you blame the controls.

FIELD NOTES

The Garden is one hundred and sixty pixels wide and one hundred and forty-four tall, and it runs at sixty steps a second. Every sprite, every letter of the on-screen text and every blade of grass is drawn from four shades of green, the same four that the original 1989 handheld could show. Nothing that appears on the screen ever leaves that palette; even the win screen is just dark green on light green.

The shell that holds it borrows the DMG's warm grey and the magenta of its two face buttons. It is not a photograph and it is not a 3D model: it is one grey box, a lot of inset shadows, a couple of rotated pills and a speaker grille made of a repeating gradient.

The game itself is tiny by design. A net, a timer and a deck of six bugs that reshuffles whenever it empties, so no species can hide from you for long. Forty-five seconds is the whole round. Ten catches is the whole goal. It is meant to be finished at a bus stop, in the time it takes for the bus to arrive, and then played once more because the beetle got away.
