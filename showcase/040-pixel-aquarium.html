    01 / Species

    Species Guide

    16 fish in tank

    Five species share the water, and none of them behave the same way. Each one is a handful of pixels with its own depth band, speed, sense radius and appetite. Press Highlight to pick one out of the crowd, or Add to drop another into the tank.

    02 / Behaviour

    How the tank thinks

    State 1Wander

    Every fish picks a point inside its preferred depth band, swims toward it, pauses, and picks another. Speed and pause length are tuned per species, so a tang loafs while a tetra flits and a goby simply sits.

    State 2Sense

    Each species has a sense radius measured in tank pixels. When a pellet enters it, and the fish is hungry enough to care, it switches state. Tetras effectively sense together, because the shoal shares one centre.

    State 3Chase

    The fish steers toward where the pellet will be, a little below where it is, because pellets sink. Faster species arrive first; hungrier fish arrive faster. A goby ignores anything that has not yet touched the sand.

    State 4Eat & rest

    The first mouth to touch the pellet takes it. Hunger resets, a few bubbles escape, and that fish ignores food for a while, so the whole tank does not become a feeding frenzy every time you click.

    1/5scale

    The tank is drawn at roughly one fifth of screen resolution — 240 × 150 internal pixels on this screen, about 130 × 280 on a phone — then stretched with image-rendering: pixelated, so every square you see is a real square. No sprite sheet is loaded: each fish is a string of seven to twelve characters painted with fillRect every frame. Kelp sways on a sine wave keyed to its own height, bubbles wobble on a private phase, and the three light shafts are drifting quadrilaterals at five percent white. Uneaten pellets dissolve into the sand after twenty-five seconds.
