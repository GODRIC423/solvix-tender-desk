Hollow Mere · Day permitSat 5 Sep · no. 0412

Thu 3 Sept clear, 9°, still

06:40Mist lying on the mere like a sheet nobody has pulled straight. The grey heron was already at the sluice, motionless — there before I was out of bed, probably.

Watched it 22 min. One lunge, one roach, swallowed head-first with the usual three shrugs. Neck folds into an S so tight the bird looks half its own height.

08:05Charm of goldfinch — 14, maybe 16 — on the thistle bank, all tinkling at once. Flight note like someone shaking loose change.

FriWind back, nothing new. Chiffchaff still saying its own name in the alders. Tea went cold.

— 3 —

Sat 5 – Sun 6 Sept warm, 18°

18:50Two swifts over the car park — high, screaming, then gone south. Last ones of the year, I think. Wrote the time down twice in case.

20:15Barn owl quartering the rough field by the pumping station. Utterly silent; you only know it is there because a piece of the dusk is paler than the rest, and moving.

Found a feather beneath its post on Sunday — buff and white, soft-edged. Kept it (see pocket).

— 4 —

Mon 31 Aug overcast, 11°, light SW

06:10Out before the dog-walkers. A wren in the bramble by the east hide, singing far too loudly for a bird the size of a walnut. Tail cocked, whole body shaking with it.

06:35Reed warblers still churring low in the reeds — later than I expected them to be here.

07:20Nothing on the water but a dozen coot and two moorhen having a slow argument about a log.

TueRain by eight. Sat in the hide and drew the wren from memory instead. The bill is finer than I keep making it, and the eye sits further forward.

Note to self: the reed warblers will be gone by the time I next write this page number. Say goodbye properly.

— 1 —

Wed 2 Sept bright, 14°

07:05The cut, below the old footbridge. I was watching a leaf fall and it was not a leaf: kingfisher, low and dead straight, a struck match going down the channel.

It landed on the willow stub 20 m off and sat for nearly four minutes. In shade the back is the colour of a bruise; when it turned into the sun it went electric. Two dives, one fish (minnow?), one miss.

07:30Second bird — or the same one back — calling tsee from the far bank. Gone by half past. Fits on one page; I will think about it for a month.

About this notebook

This is the seventh in a run of pocket field books, all the same: full-grain leather over stiff boards, saddle-stitched with waxed linen, brass corners so the boards survive being sat on, and a wide elastic to keep the whole thing shut in a coat pocket when the wind gets into the reeds. The paper is a heavy cream laid stock that takes a soft pencil without shine and forgives the occasional splash from the mere.

The notes are written in the field, in the moment, and left unedited — times, weather, and whatever was seen or heard, with sketches made from life where the bird sat still long enough and from memory where it did not. The tally at the end of each week is transferred to the county recorder; the notebook itself stays in the drawer, which is the point of it.

Hollow Mere reserve — the mere, the cut below the footbridge, the east hide, the thistle bank and the rough field by the pumping station.
