Notes from the concourse

01 — MechanismForty-one flaps, one drum

Each character you see is a self-contained module: a stack of forty-one hinged plastic flaps on a horizontal drum, each flap carrying the bottom half of one letter and the top half of the next. A stepper motor turns the drum one detent at a time and an optical index tells the controller where the alphabet currently sits.

The board cannot jump. To show a Z when it is resting on a C, it must fall through all the letters in between — which is why a whole board changing at once sounds like rain on a metal roof, and why nobody who has heard it wants a screen instead.

02 — RhythmFifty-six milliseconds

One flap falls in fifty-six milliseconds. The modules are wired in columns rather than rows, so a full board refresh starts at the left edge and travels right, arriving as a wave rather than a bang. The stagger is not decorative: driving six hundred motors simultaneously would brown out the cabinet.

Turn on Clack above and the board gets its voice back. It is off by default, because at four in the morning this hall is the quietest room in the country and we would like to keep it that way.
