The ten stalls สิบร้าน

Numbered west to east, canal to gate. Heat is the stall's signature dish as it comes; every vendor will go up or down if you ask.

  1. 1

    Pa Noi · 31 years on this corner

    Nam dok mai mango over warm coconut sticky rice, salted coconut cream, a scatter of crisp mung beans.

    Pa Noi cuts the mango table-side and refuses to open before the season's fruit is right, so if her awning is up, the mangoes are good.

    ฿80
  2. 2

    Lung Dam · cast-iron mould, charcoal only

    Coconut-rice pancakes with a custardy centre and a crisp shell, topped with spring onion, sweet corn or taro.

    Listen for the tap of the spatula on iron. That is how you find him before you see him.

    ฿30 / 8 pieces
  3. 3

    Ae & Nong · one wok, one roaring flame

    Rice noodles tossed over red-hot charcoal with tamarind, dried shrimp and tofu, wrapped in a lacy egg net.

    Chili comes on the side in a jar of roasted flakes; the noodles themselves are gentle, smoky and a little sweet.

    ฿70
  4. 4

    Jae Ann · grill lit at four sharp

    Pork skewers marinated in coconut milk, garlic and palm sugar, charred over coals and sold with a bag of hot sticky rice.

    The dipping jaew carries a faint warmth, mostly smoke, lime and toasted rice. Buy more than you think you want.

    ฿12 / skewer
  5. 5

    Ah Tee · at the khlong's edge

    Small bowls of dark pork broth thickened with blood, rice noodles, pork crackling, morning glory and holy basil.

    He sits where the boats used to tie up. Stack your empty bowls in a tower; regulars manage eight before the broth runs out.

    ฿20 / bowl
  6. 6

    The Rot Fai family · third generation

    Poached chicken over ginger-and-garlic rice, a clear bowl of broth, and the sauce: fermented soybean, ginger, vinegar, chili.

    Mild until you spoon on the sauce, which brings a proper mid-table heat. The chicken skin is the point.

    ฿55
  7. 7

    Mae Jan · pounded to order

    Green papaya bruised in a clay mortar with lime, palm sugar, fish sauce, dried shrimp, peanuts and as many chilies as you name.

    Say a number. Mae Jan counts the chilies into the mortar in front of you and does not flinch. Three is the house default.

    ฿50
  8. 8

    Sao Isan · Udon Thani recipes

    Minced pork larb and sliced grilled-beef nam tok, both dressed with toasted rice powder, dried chili, shallot and mint.

    Northeastern heat: dry, toasted and slow to build. Order sticky rice and raw cabbage to fill the gaps between bites.

    ฿70
  9. 9

    Phi Aor · river prawns from Ayutthaya

    Creamy tom yum with head-on river prawns, lemongrass, galangal, makrut lime, evaporated milk and bird's-eye chilies bruised whole.

    Sour, sweet and violently hot at once. The milk does not soften it; it only makes everything taste bigger.

    ฿120
  10. 10

    Bang Yai · the southern stall by the gate

    Southern dry-fried minced pork with a fresh turmeric curry paste and a snowfall of hair-thin shredded makrut lime leaf.

    Nothing on this map is hotter. The plate arrives with cucumber, long beans and raw cabbage for a reason.

    ฿60

Order by heat สั่งตามความเผ็ด

The chips above the map use the same five words the vendors do. Say one of them with a smile and a raised hand and you will be understood at every stall on this map.

  1. ไม่เผ็ดmai phet no chili at all; the sauce still comes on the side
  2. เผ็ดน้อยphet noi a little; one chili, or flakes you add yourself
  3. เผ็ดกลางphet klang medium; what most Bangkok tables order
  4. เผ็ดphet hot; the vendor's own default
  5. เผ็ดมากphet mak very hot; you will be asked if you are sure
