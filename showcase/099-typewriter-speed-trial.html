Operator’s notes

This bench measures one thing honestly: how many clean words a hand can put on paper in a minute. A word is five characters, spaces counted, exactly as the typing schools have reckoned it since the first speed trials were held in shop windows to sell machines.

Set your fingers on the home row and begin. The clock starts on the first key, not before, so there is no penalty for reading the passage twice. Strike a wrong letter and the ribbon shifts to red: the mistake is overprinted where it fell, because a typewriter has no undoing, only correction. Backspace walks the carriage back and leaves a pale erasure, but the error stays on your record.

When the carriage reaches the right margin the bell rings once and the platen turns the paper up a line. Listen for it — a practised typist returns on the bell without ever looking up. At the end of the passage the machine stamps your rate on the sheet in red. Rest the wrists. Then take a fresh sheet.
