I · The House

A Welcome, Written in Brass

Guests arrive beneath a canopy of stepped bronze, into a lobby whose sunburst floor was laid by hand in Verde Antique marble and forty-one shades of gold leaf. The doormen have worn the same emerald livery since opening night; the elevators still announce each floor with a brass chime tuned to E-flat, the note our founder believed to be the sound of good fortune.

The Meridian was built to be the exact centre of the city's evening. It sits where the two great avenues cross, and its tower was set so that on the summer solstice the sun descends precisely down the axis of the ballroom windows. Everything here was made to be symmetrical, and nothing was made to be hurried.

Two hundred and twelve rooms, three suites in the crown, a bar on the forty-first floor, and a staff who will remember how you take your coffee for the rest of your life.
