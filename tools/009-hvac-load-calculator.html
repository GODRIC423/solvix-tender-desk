Preliminary rule-of-thumb figure — this is not a Manual J. It multiplies floor area by a BTU-per-square-foot index and applies fixed percentage adjustments. It does not model orientation, shading, infiltration, duct losses, solar or latent gain, or the actual construction of your walls and roof. Do not submit it for a permit and do not select equipment from it. A full room-by-room ACCA Manual J load calculation — with Manual S for equipment selection and Manual D for duct design, or an equivalent recognised method such as BS EN 12831 — must be carried out by a qualified HVAC designer before anything is ordered or installed.

Heating and cooling are shown from one index. This rule of thumb produces a single BTU/h figure, and it is weighted towards heating — the base index rises as the climate gets colder. Converting it straight to tons will usually overstate the cooling capacity a dwelling needs, and an oversized cooling unit short-cycles and fails to dehumidify. Treat the tonnage as an upper bound for budgeting only.

HVAC Rough Load Sizing — implements the BTU-per-square-foot rule of thumb exactly as stated: base index 30/35/40/45/50/55/60 BTU/h·ft² for climate zones 1–7, insulation +25%/0/−15%, glazing +15%/0/−10%, +600 BTU/h per occupant over two, +4,000 BTU/h per kitchen, tons = BTU/h ÷ 12,000. Zone names follow the broad IECC climate zone bands; Great Britain and Ireland sit broadly in zone 5. Assumes a conditioned dwelling of ordinary ceiling height with no allowance for orientation, shading, infiltration, duct loss or latent gain. It is not a substitute for an ACCA Manual J (or BS EN 12831) calculation and must not be used for permit submission or equipment selection. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded.
