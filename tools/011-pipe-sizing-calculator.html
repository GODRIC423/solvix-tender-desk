Local plumbing code governs — this is a design aid, not an approval. Sizes here come from the fixture-unit method (Hunter's curve for supply demand, DFU capacity tables for drainage) as published in the International Plumbing Code appendices. Your jurisdiction may adopt the UPC, NSPC, a state amendment or a different table, and may impose minimum sizes, pressure ranges, water-conservation limits or well/booster rules this tool does not model. It does not size water heaters, pumps, recirculation, fire lines, gas or storm drainage. Have every design checked by a licensed plumber or engineer and confirm it with the authority having jurisdiction before you order material or cut pipe.

What this implements. Water supply fixture units per fixture from IPC Appendix E Table E103.3(2) (private use); total WSFU converted to peak demand in gpm by linear interpolation of Hunter's probability curve (IPC Table E103.3(3), flush-tank and flushometer-valve columns); pipe bore from d = √(0.4085·Q ÷ v) with v the erosion velocity limit, rounded up to the next stocked nominal size using published internal diameters; friction loss by Hazen–Williams h = 4.52·Q^1.852 ÷ (C^1.852·d^4.8655) psi/ft; static head at 0.4335 psi per foot of rise. Drainage fixture units per IPC Table 709.1 (private use); horizontal fixture branch capacity per IPC Table 710.1(2) (1½ in = 3 DFU, 2 in = 6, 2½ in = 12, 3 in = 20, 4 in = 160, 5 in = 360, 6 in = 620); building drain and sewer capacity by slope per IPC Table 710.1(1); a 3 in minimum wherever a water closet discharges. Vents sized at not less than half the diameter of the drain served and never less than 1¼ in (IPC 903.1 / 916.2), with a 3 in minimum through the roof where frost closure applies. Assumes cold potable water near 60 °F, a single building, no booster or pressure-reducing valve unless you enter its loss, and no simultaneous continuous-flow demand.
