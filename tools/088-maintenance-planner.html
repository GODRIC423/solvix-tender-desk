This is a scheduling and tracking tool, not a compliance certificate. It calculates next-due dates from the frequencies and dates you enter — it does not know the statutory or manufacturer-required interval for any asset, does not inspect anything, and does not verify that work was carried out correctly. For fire, lift/LOLER, gas, electrical, pressure equipment or other regulated maintenance, confirm the required interval with the manufacturer, your insurer or a competent person, and keep the original certificates separately from this record.

Planned Maintenance Schedule. Next due date = last completed date advanced by the task's frequency (daily +1 day, weekly +7 days, monthly +1 calendar month, quarterly +3 months, biannual +6 months, annual +12 months, or every N days). A task never marked completed is treated as due now. Days overdue is today minus the next due date; the schedule always lists overdue tasks first, most overdue at the top, then due today, then due soonest. Recording a completion logs whether it was on or before the date it was due, sets that date as the new "last completed", and rolls the next due date forward — the compliance percentage is completions on time divided by all logged completions. The workload forecast sums each task's estimated hours into the week its occurrence falls in over the next twelve weeks, starting overdue tasks in the current week; a week is flagged when it exceeds your stated weekly capacity. Everything is stored in this browser only — nothing is uploaded, and clearing your browser data deletes it.
