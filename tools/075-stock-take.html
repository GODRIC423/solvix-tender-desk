Usage = opening + purchases − closing. Expected = sales ÷ pack size. A positive variance means more stock went than the till sold.

Variance is an indicator, not proof. A gap between counted usage and till sales can come from miscounts and part-unit estimates, wastage or line cleaning, unrecorded transfers between outlets, deliveries booked into the wrong period, promotions and staff drinks not rung through, or till errors — as easily as from over-pouring or theft. Recount the worst lines, check delivery notes and wastage records, and confirm the pack sizes before you raise it with anyone. Figures are management information at purchase cost, not an audited stock valuation; take advice from your accountant before using them in accounts or in any disciplinary process.

Order quantities are rounded up to whole units to bring stock back to par.

Usage = opening stock + purchases − closing stock, in counted units. Expected usage = sales servings ÷ pack size (servings per counted unit). Variance = actual usage − expected usage, valued at the purchase cost you enter; variance % is measured against expected usage. Days of cover = closing units ÷ (usage ÷ days between counts). Stock is valued at purchase cost on the basis you select, not at retail or at net realisable value, and no stock-valuation or accounting standard is applied. Part units are the counter's estimate to the nearest tenth. Everything stays in this browser.
