A reading log and a cost estimate — not a bill. It prices the readings you enter at the rates you type in. It does not know your supplier's tariff, VAT treatment or billing periods, so check every figure against the actual bill. Gas volume becomes kWh using the calorific value you enter — the real figure varies daily by region and is printed on your gas bill. A flag means only that this period used more per day than recent periods; the cause has to be found by inspection. If you smell gas or suspect a gas escape, leave the building and call your national gas emergency number. Faulty meters and disputed readings are for your supplier or a qualified engineer.

A period is flagged when its daily average (billing units per day) exceeds the mean daily average of the trailing periods by more than the threshold. Comparing per-day figures means unequal gaps between readings do not create false alarms. Seasonal swings can: heating through a cold month genuinely doubles gas use, so widen the threshold or the window on heating meters rather than treating every rise as a fault.

Consumption = current reading − previous reading, times the dial multiplier. Gas volume is converted to energy with the standard industry formula kWh = m³ × volume correction factor (1.02264) × calorific value (MJ/m³) ÷ 3.6; imperial gas dials are converted at 1 unit of 100 ft³ = 2.83 m³ and 1 ft³ = 0.0283168466 m³. Period cost = billable quantity × unit rate + days × standing charge, plus the tax percentage you set. Days are counted between reading dates. An anomaly is raised when a period's daily average exceeds the trailing mean by your chosen percentage. All figures are your own estimate from your own inputs — the supplier's bill is the authoritative document.
