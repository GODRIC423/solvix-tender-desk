What this is, and what it is not. This document compares the cash cost of taking an early‑payment discount against the cash cost of borrowing for the same number of days, using the terms and rates entered by the preparer. It is an arithmetic working, not financial, accounting, credit or tax advice. It ignores VAT and other taxes, bank arrangement and drawdown fees, contractual late‑payment interest and statutory interest, credit limits, bad‑debt and collection costs, supplier relationships, and any covenant or cash‑flow constraint on your business. Figures assume every party pays exactly on the day modelled. Confirm the terms against the supplier’s contract or invoice and discuss any material decision with your accountant or finance director before acting on it.

Effective annual rate uses the standard trade‑credit convention: (d ÷ (100 − d)) × (365 ÷ (net days − discount days)), simple interest on a 365‑day year with no compounding. The compounded figure restates the same period rate as (1 + d ÷ (100 − d))^(365 ÷ T) − 1. Borrowing cost is simple interest on the discounted amount for the days of credit given up. Seller figures assume every customer pays exactly on the day modelled and ignore bad debt, collection cost and tax.
