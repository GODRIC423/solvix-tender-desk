Check the plan against the actual timber before you cut. This tool packs the lengths you enter using first-fit-decreasing bin packing with one saw kerf per cut. It assumes straight, defect-free stock at exactly the nominal lengths given, and that any board may be cut in any order. It makes no allowance for bow, crook, split or waney ends, grain and face selection, matching, or offcuts already in your rack — add an end-trim allowance and extra quantity for those yourself. Measure and mark out every board before cutting. Prices are as entered and exclude tax and delivery. Solvix accepts no liability for material ordered or cut from this plan.

Bars are drawn to scale against the longest stock length. Solid blocks are finished parts, the hatched block is the offcut left on that board, and the thin dark lines are the saw kerf lost at each cut. Identical boards are grouped.

Notice. Plan generated by first-fit-decreasing bin packing with one saw kerf per cut. It assumes straight, defect-free stock at the nominal lengths entered and makes no allowance for bow, crook, split ends, face or grain selection, or existing offcuts. Measure and mark out every board before cutting. Prices as entered, excluding tax and delivery.
Method. Pieces are sorted longest-first and packed by first-fit-decreasing bin packing, charging the saw kerf against every cut made. The pack is run once for each stock length on its own, and mixed across all lengths by several rules — opening each new board in the length with the lowest cost per unit of finished timber, then downgrading every board to the cheapest length that still holds what was packed on it. The cheapest plan that cuts every piece is the one shown, so a plan with more boards can win when it costs less. Mixed-length packing against a price list has no exact shortcut, so treat the mixed plan as a strong candidate rather than a proven optimum — the strategies table shows what it was measured against. A board holding k pieces of total length S needs S + kerf × (k−1) ≤ length − end trim. Waste % = (total stock length bought − total cut length) ÷ total stock length bought, counting kerf loss, end trim and offcut. Quantities always round up to whole boards; money is shown to 2 dp. Not a substitute for checking the timber itself.
