This is a cost–volume–profit model, not accounting, tax or investment advice. It assumes selling prices, variable cost per unit and total fixed costs stay constant across the whole volume range, that the sales mix stays as entered, and that everything produced is sold in the period. Real businesses break those assumptions: discounts, bulk buying, overtime, stepped fixed costs, stock movements and seasonality all shift the break-even point. Figures are stated excluding VAT and any other sales tax, and exclude interest, depreciation policy choices, drawings, corporation or income tax and anything not typed into this sheet. Check the inputs against your own bookkeeping records and take advice from a qualified accountant before relying on these numbers to price work, borrow, or commit to costs.

Cost–volume–profit (CVP) break-even model. Contribution per unit = price − variable cost per unit; break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution; break-even revenue = break-even units × price; contribution margin ratio = contribution ÷ price; margin of safety = (forecast sales − break-even sales) ÷ forecast sales; target-profit units = (fixed costs + target profit) ÷ contribution. With more than one line the contribution is weighted by the forecast sales mix. Linear costs and revenue are assumed throughout, volumes are rounded up to whole units, money is shown to 2 decimal places, and all figures exclude VAT and other sales taxes.
