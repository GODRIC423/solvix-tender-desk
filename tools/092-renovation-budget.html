Retention is a percentage of each stage payment that you hold back until the defects period ends. It is money still owed — it is not a saving. Anything you buy or pay for outside the building contract appears in the cost plan only, so the schedule total and the forecast final cost will usually differ.

How every figure is worked out. Base budget = the sum of the budget column. Contingency pot = base budget × contingency %. Total approved budget = base budget + contingency pot. Line forecast = the actual cost when the line is marked 100% done and an actual has been entered, otherwise the greater of the actual spent so far and the current commitment (the quote, or the budget where there is no quote). Line variance = forecast − budget; positive means over. Forecast final cost = the sum of the line forecasts; the contingency pot is not added to it, because contingency only becomes real cost once it lands on a line. Variance vs approved = forecast final cost − total approved budget. Contingency remaining = pot − the draw-down log. Overspend not yet drawn = the total of all positive line variances − the draw-down log, floored at zero. Work complete = Σ(line forecast × line done %) ÷ Σ(line forecast), so expensive work counts for more. Spent = actual to date ÷ total approved budget. Materials = forecast × materials %, labour is the remainder. Net due on a stage payment = gross × (1 − retention %). Paid out = the net of every stage marked paid. A category alert fires when a room’s or a trade’s variance is more than the alert threshold % of that category’s budget, and a payment alert fires when a stage is unpaid after its due date. All money is rounded to 2 decimal places at line level before totalling, so the columns add up. Empty or invalid figures count as zero and show as —. Everything is saved only in this browser; nothing is uploaded. Not professional cost, contract, tax or legal advice.
