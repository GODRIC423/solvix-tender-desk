This is a statement, not a set of accounts and not a legal demand. It shows the transactions you enter, on the dates you give them, aged against the due dates you set. It does not reconcile to your bookkeeping system, does not know about disputed items, retentions, tax points or credit insurance, and it does not calculate statutory late-payment interest or compensation. Check the figures against your sales ledger before you send it, and take advice from your accountant or a debt-recovery professional before relying on it in a dispute or a claim.

Ageing follows the standard open-item convention: each unpaid debit document sits in the bucket set by its own due date measured against the “age as at” date — current (not yet due), 1–30, 31–60, 61–90 and 90+ days overdue — and the five buckets always add back to the closing balance. Cash and credit notes are matched to a named document where you say so, and otherwise oldest-first. Money is rounded to two decimal places at entry and all allocation arithmetic is done in whole minor units, so the columns cannot drift. Everything is stored in this browser only; nothing is sent anywhere.
